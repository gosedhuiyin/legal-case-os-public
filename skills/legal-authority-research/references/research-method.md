# Authority Research Method

## Plan

Define:

- decision question and proposed proposition;
- jurisdiction, time, forum, court level, and procedural posture;
- element, burden, remedy, or procedural consequence at issue;
- favorable and adverse vocabulary, factual synonyms, and citation chains;
- read and network scope.

## Search, read, and reflect

Use a user-configured read-only knowledge base, selected local files or uploads within current authorization; public internet permission is separate. With sufficient supplied sources, direct reading can replace discovery. Backend failure limits coverage, not the ability to compare the actual supplied material. Follow [network degradation](../../../shared/policies/network-degradation.md).

1. Prefer current official legislation, judicial interpretations, court rules, and full-text judgments or authoritative publications.
2. Read the relevant full text and surrounding context; a search result or AI summary is not verification.
3. Compare facts, posture, burden, issue, reasoning, disposition and relevant validity. Separate court findings, party arguments and author commentary; preserve attributed conditional opinions for comparison rather than treating every useful statement as a holding.
4. Record both support and material conflict. Explain distinctions instead of hiding adverse authority.
5. Create a follow-up query only when a defined gap remains.

## Stop and degrade

Stop with `verification_status=verified` when controlling authority and the material adverse landscape are sufficiently supported. Use `unverified` when access or verification remains incomplete and `failed` when a claimed record cannot be substantiated; record the precise access limit and workflow degradation.

Never say “no adverse case exists”; say what sources and scope were searched and what was not found. An unavailable paid source does not authorize fabricated results.

Sources labeled TEST-ONLY may support synthetic workflow tests and explicitly synthetic analysis only. Production mode must reject them from any filing or court-candidate artifact.
